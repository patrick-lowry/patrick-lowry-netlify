## The Statistics Are In

I did some investigative journalism (aka stalked everyone on LinkedIn during a particularly boring lecture) and here's what I found:

### My Cohort Breakdown:
- **Mathematics degrees**: 17 people
- **Computer Science**: 5 people
- **Physics**: 3 people
- **Engineering**: 2 people
- **"Other" (me)**: 1 person

That's right. I'm the "Other" category. I'm a whole category unto myself.

## The Problem

Every single lecture assumes you know things like:

- What a derivative is (and not just conceptually, but like... intimately)
- Matrix operations (beyond "that thing Neo dodged")
- Probability theory (apparently flipping a coin is more complicated than I thought)
- Eigenvalues (still not convinced these are real)

The professor will casually say things like "as we all know from basic linear algebra" and I'm sitting there thinking "bold of you to assume."

## The Math People

I've started categorizing my classmates into subcategories:

### The Naturals
These people just... understand math. They see an equation and their eyes light up like normal people looking at puppies. One of them actually said "that's a beautiful proof" unironically.

### The Former Mathmos
Cambridge graduates who speak in a dialect I don't understand. They use words like "trivial" and "obvious" for things that are definitely neither.

### The Applied Math Folks
They come from engineering or physics. At least they sometimes struggle too, but usually only because they're trying to decide which of the three different advanced techniques to use.

## My Survival Strategy

I've realized I need to either:

1. Learn several years of mathematics in a few weeks (unlikely)
2. Get very good at nodding and looking confident while frantically Googling under the desk
3. Accept that "fake it till you make it" is now my entire education strategy

I'm going with option 2, with a healthy dose of option 3.

## The Upside

At least when I do eventually understand something, it feels like an actual achievement. When the math people get something right, it's Tuesday. When I get something right, it's a minor miracle and I'm telling everyone about it.

Also, I'm pretty sure I'm going to come out of this program with either a deep understanding of machine learning or the world's most expensive anxiety disorder. Maybe both!

*Day 5 and still surviving...*
