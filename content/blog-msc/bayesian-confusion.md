## Choose your poison
The course was divided into 3 terms - Terms 1 and 2 were for lectures and 8 weeks long each (with a 'reading week' after 4 weeks). Term 3 was for Exams and your final project. Every student had to do 8 modules across the first 2 terms, although how many in each term was up to them as long as they did 8 in total.

Within 2 weeks of starting each Term you had to commit to what modules you were doing, but apart from the material on the public website, there was no additional information available. Although some of the module leads had gone to the trouble of preparing a short video on their course, many hadn't bothered. So, naturally everybody spent the first two weeks cramming into as many lectures as possible before making their minds up.

In retrospect, I think there was an element of intellectual snobbery at work - many people wanted to do "Advanced Machine Learning" simply becuase it was 'Advanced'! I initialy fell for this, but when I fell asleep in the first lecture (it was over Teams and I'd just had a big lunch!), I decided this one wasn't for me! 

In any case I had 2 mandatory courses in Term 1 - 'Introduction to Statistics' (yawn) and then a choice between either 'Introduction to Machine Learning' or 'Supervised Learning'. Supervised Learning is fundamental to how machines actually 'learn' and I was initially drawn to that from the some of the preparatlry readin I'd done. However, the course prosectus did come with a warning : students must "To be eligible to select this module as an optional or elective, a student must ..... have high competency with Multivariable Calculus, Probability and Combinatorics, and Linear Algebra such that they can reprove basic results as well as novel results." Emmmm...... what do they mean by 'high competency'. I mean, I'd consider myself pretty good at calculus, probability and linear algebra. I got an 'A' in Maths at school, had an Engineering degree and had spent the last few weeks swotting up - how hard can it be.

So I joined the first lecture in Supervised Learning. It was fine initially; there was a good introduction to the topic (tick!), an overview of the course objectives (tick!), more detail on the pre-requisite knowledge and where to find it (tick!). There was also an accompanying Powerpoint (double tick!). The issue was, the 2 lectures of week 1 extended to 71 pages of Powerpoint and by only slide 27 we were already onto slides containing ONLY mathematical forumlae - ooops!

Needless to say, I swallowed my pride and opted for 'Introduction to Machine Learning' instead :-)

## Bayesian Confusion
Having selected my optional module and being required to take Statistics (yawn) I only had to choose 2 more modules. One was a no-brainer - 'Machine Learning in Biomedicine and Healthcare', a mix of technical and non-techincal which was fully mared via coursework - so no exams!!

The final one I wanted to do was called 'Bayesian Deep Learning'.

Probability and Bayes Theorem underlies much of Machine Learning - in fact, in classification (is this picture a picture of a cat or a dog) the Machine Learning algorithm is predicting the probability of each option. Bayes Law allows you to answer questions like "If Manchester United beat Liverpool today, whats the probability that today is Sunday". I enjoyed learning this prior to the MSc and wanted to learn more about it - especialy since the lecturer got rave reviews and previous students had been very positive about the module.

I attended the lectures and must admit I found it hard. The lecturer was great and I could roughly follow what he was saying, but then he'd throw in a mathematical term that I simply didn't understand and I'd be completely lost. The word "affine" was a great example - if there was a Top 10 of mathematical terms used, "affine" would be the one of that stays at the top of the charts for 15 weeks. I had no clue what this even meant - I mean, why would I know that? I'm not a mathematician, I haven't just completed an undergad in Maths from f**king Cambridge University. I can tell you how to quickly format a Powerpoint, write a Business Case or refactor a 20 year old monolithic eCommerce site. I can even find the spell check in Excel - but none of the above have ever required me to know what the word 'affine' means!

I soldiered on

This course was mostly online. In fact, the lecturer would release videos every week (recorded during Covid); you'd be expected to have watched them and understood them prior to in-person sessions the following week. This was a good approach in theory which resonated strongly with my sense of structure and natural tendency towards systematic ways of thnking (to the frustration and amusement of others). But the in-person sessions were not teaching sessions at all, they were Q&A sessions. The lecturer might run quickly through the material but the focus was primarliy on giving studetns an oportunity to ask specific questions abuot the material. These sesseions were dominated by recent Maths graduates asking quite advanced theoretical questions - whereas I was still on Maths 101! 

However, with the videos available, I decided that it was within my gift to take the time to understand them, by week 2 it was getting a bit better and I was determined to keep going. 

## A False Dawn
There were 4 hours of video lectures for week 3 - these were released on Wednesday of week 2. With the final course selection due that Friday I took myself to the library and started watching the videos. It probably took me 2 hours to get through the first hour-long video on Thursday. The second one was a bit easier - it only took 1 and 20mins. I didn't have time to look at the other 2 before finalising my course selecton but, feeling that I was on an upward trajectory I submitted my course preference that Friday to include Bayesian Deep Learning. I went for a deeply satisfying beer after my last Friday lecture and decided I was going to go into the library on Saturday morning to do the other 2 videos.

## Reality Bites
Oh. My. God.

I arrived in the library on Saturday morning and started up video 3. I'm not joking, it took me the whole morning to get through it. Again, it was the random mathematical terms that were thrown in that I'd never really heard before. There was one 5 minute period where I must have been stopping the video every 15 or 30 seconds to google a term. I'd often have to look up a few sources to make sure I understood it in the context of the lecture video. Then I'd rewind the video, replay that section, pause it, take notes and progress another 15 seconds, maybe 30 if I was lucky. Then I'd have to stop to look up another mathematical term again. It was EXTREMELY painful.

At 4pm my brain was mush and nothing was going in. I had 30mins of video left which I could do on Sunday.


## Reality Check
But as so happens, after a good nights sleep I got a different perspective. I had spent nearly 8 hours going through just one week of material for just 1 course. I also had Statistics (yawn) to think about - with its own Powerpoint slides (100 on avcerage per week), vidoes, lectures and exercises every week. Then I also had 'Introduction to Machine Learning' - also with its own videos, powerpoints (at least 100 slides per week) 1 hour of lectures and a 3 hour lab every week. 

This was not sustainable.

On Monday morning I immediately headed up to the admin office and, bearing the gift of a box of Lindt Chocloate Eggs purchsed from the Student Unioin shop, pleaded with them to let me swap out. Fortunately they agreed. Phew!!! What a relief. What a great decision that was!
